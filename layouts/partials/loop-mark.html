A visual pun for "Stay in the loop" — the elevated tracks that circle the Chicago Loop, drawn the way the CTA rail diagrams draw them: fat rounded strokes running concentrically, portrait rather than square, because the real Loop is taller than it is wide. Three rings, concentric on (65.5, 75) and inset 14 apart. The stroke is 9, so that leaves a 5px gap — wide enough that they read as three tracks rather than one thick band. The outer ring is 84 x 112, and everything else falls out of that: the innermost ends up 28 wide, which is exactly twice its corner radius, so it closes into a stadium the way the poster's inner track does. The white track is a backwards C that goes through the mark rather than around it: in along the bottom, up through the loop's bottom edge on the centreline, and back out to the left at y=75 — the rings' exact mid-height, so the upper run looks aimed rather than approximate. It is drawn as arc, straight, arc rather than as one semicircle, and that is what makes the entry readable. A semicircle between two runs 70 apart has to have a radius of 35, which is wider than the rings and swings the turn outside them; splitting it lets the radius drop to 14 and puts a straight climb in the middle, so the track crosses the bottom edge square on and rises far enough to surface inside the innermost ring before it peels away. The crossings are the point. It passes under the bottom of all three rings going up and under the left of all three coming out, surfacing only in the gaps. A track that runs under something reads as track; the same line laid over the top would read as a strike-through. That crossing, and the red one, are the reason this is a rail diagram and not a zero. Both are drawn before the rings so the rings weave over them, and both run past the rings to the edge of the canvas, so the mark reads as a detail crop of something larger. The vertical one is red, and it has to be lightened and run at high alpha to stay that way. Red and teal are close in value and near-opposite in hue, so red laid over this gradient at the white line's opacity goes brown — it desaturates toward the ground before it fades. Matching the two by opacity would not have matched them by weight; the red is set by eye against the teal instead. The canvas is 113 wide because the track no longer sets the right edge — the outer ring does. The two runs bleed off at x=0, so the ink spans the viewBox in both directions and centres on its own.
